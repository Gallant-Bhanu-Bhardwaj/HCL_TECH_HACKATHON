## 1. Scope

This rule lists the situations that are handed to a human desk rather
than answered or actioned by the chat assistant, and what the hand-over
must include.

## 2. Desks

| Desk | Handles |
|---|---|
| complaints   | General service complaints not covered below |
| refunds      | Disputed or missing refunds, delay-related refund claims (RUL-OPC-01 §3) |
| safety       | Safety or harassment reports on board or at a station |
| concessions  | Concession claims the assistant cannot verify from the booking record (RUL-CON-01 §3) |
| general      | Anything that needs a person but does not fit the above |

## 3. What every hand-over includes

A hand-over always includes a short summary of the issue, the relevant
booking ID (if any), and what the assistant already told the
passenger. It never includes a promise of a specific outcome, refund
amount, or timeline — only staff at that desk can decide those.

## 4. When the assistant must hand over

- The passenger explicitly asks for a person.
- A safety or harassment report, regardless of how it is phrased.
- A refund or charge dispute ("I was charged the wrong amount").
- A concession claim the assistant cannot verify (RUL-CON-01 §3).
- A delay-based refund claim (RUL-OPC-01 §3), since Ujjwal Rail's
  systems here do not hold live delay data.
- The assistant genuinely cannot answer with confidence after one
  clarifying question.

## 5. When the assistant must NOT hand over

A question the assistant can answer directly from a rules document or
a tool — a train search, a fare, a standard refund quote, or a plain
policy question — is answered directly. Handing over an answerable
question is treated as a quality failure, not a safe default.

## 6. Response to an out-of-scope request

A request unrelated to Ujjwal Rail travel (for example, booking a
hotel) is politely refused, not handed to a desk — there is no desk
for it.
